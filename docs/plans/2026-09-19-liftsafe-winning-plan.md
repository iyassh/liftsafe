## 1. The idea, upgraded

**Today:** a lifting check a worker does once a quarter. It works, but a product used four times a year gets cancelled.

**Proposed:** a **daily, camera-verified, 3-minute shift-start routine**, with the quarterly lift check as the certification on top.

1. Worker walks up to a tablet at the start of shift and checks in.
2. One tap: "Any soreness today?" (body area, or none).
3. Three short movements the camera verifies were actually done, and done well (for example: overhead reach, five squats, hip hinge).
4. "Shift-ready ✓", a timestamp, and a streak.
5. The supervisor's dashboard shows who checked in today, participation, streaks, soreness flags, and who is due for the quarterly lift certification.

**Customizable by business:** movements are configuration, not code. A "Warehouse pack", a "Movers pack", a "Retail backroom pack" are different lists of the same building blocks. This is the platform story: the engine is general; lifting was the first module.

**One line:** *LiftSafe is the 3-minute, camera-verified shift-start routine for physical jobs. Workers warm up and practise safe movement daily, and the employer gets proof it happened.*

## 2. What the research says (read this before writing any slide)

### 2.1 The inconvenient evidence, and why it helps us

- A **Cochrane systematic review** (18 studies, over 20,000 workers) found that manual handling training **does not prevent back pain**. Workers liked the training and their knowledge improved, but injuries did not drop.
- The evidence that **pre-shift stretching** reduces injuries is **inconclusive**. Some company programs report lower injury rates; reviews say the studies are weak.

A judge with a safety background may know this. If we claim "our training reduces injuries", we lose them. So we do not claim it. We use it:

> "Companies spend on lifting training that is proven not to work. It fails for an obvious reason: a video once a year, no practice, no feedback, no follow-up. Nobody learns a physical skill that way. LiftSafe is the opposite: real practice, individual feedback, every day."

**What we promise** is only what we can measure: participation, technique scores over time, documented training, early soreness flags. **What we will prove** is injury reduction, with pilot customers. Investors respect a clear hypothesis and a plan to test it far more than a claim they can puncture.

### 2.2 The money

| Fact | Use |
|---|---|
| MSIs are 30% of WorkSafeBC time-loss claims; ~88,000 claims, $2.35B over 2020–2024 | Problem slide |
| Average BC time-loss claim ≈ $43,000 direct (secondary source; say "approximately"); indirect costs ≈ 2× on top | Cost of one injury |
| WorkSafeBC **experience rating**: claims history moves an employer's premium up or down | Why owners care |
| **COR** (Certificate of Recognition): up to **10% premium rebate** for an audited safety program | Concrete ROI; our records support the audit |
| BC OHS Regulation 4.46–4.53: employers must assess MSI risk, train exposed workers, review annually | "You already have to do this" |
| Warehouse turnover ≈ 49% a year (US BLS figure, via industry sources); replacing an hourly worker costs thousands | Constant new hires = constant need for onboarding |

### 2.3 Market size

- Top-down: the **AI workplace ergonomics market is estimated at ~US$1.9B (2024), growing ~18% a year to ~US$8.6B by 2033.** These are market-research-firm estimates; say "estimated".
- Bottom-up (stronger, investors prefer it): **workers in physical jobs × price per worker.** BC: construction is ~9% of employment and manufacturing ~6%, plus transportation/warehousing and retail. **TODO (research owner):** pull exact headcounts from the BC Stats "Employment by Industry" table and compute BC → Canada → North America at $60 per worker per year.

### 2.4 Competitors

| Who | What they do | Gap we fill |
|---|---|---|
| **Vimocity** | "Workforce readiness" and pre-shift warm-ups for utilities; claims 40–50% injury reduction | Enterprise sales; video content. Nobody checks the worker actually did the movement, or how well |
| **TuMeke, Soter, Kinetica** | AI video ergonomic *assessments* for safety professionals | A tool for ergonomists at big companies. Not worker training, not daily, not self-serve |
| **SafetyCulture, Safesite** ($16–24 per user/month) | Checklists, inspections, toolbox talks | Paperwork, not movement. No camera, no coaching |
| Posters, annual video, shadowing | What small businesses do today | Proven not to work |

**Our position:** verified (camera, not honour system) · daily (habit, not annual) · self-serve for small business (no sales call, no consultant) · private (on-device, no video stored).

### 2.5 Legal and ethical guardrails (these protect the pitch)

- **Never gate work on a movement score.** Refusing to let someone clock in because of how they move is a human-rights and disability-discrimination risk. LiftSafe coaches and records; it never blocks a shift.
- **Do not build a time clock.** Payroll-grade timekeeping is crowded and regulated. We log a "shift-start check-in" timestamp; integration with real time clocks is roadmap.
- **The warm-up is paid time.** A mandatory activity is work. Say so: "three paid minutes".
- **Privacy (BC PIPA):** employers need a reasonable purpose, notice and consent, and the least intrusive method. On-device processing with no video stored is exactly that. Keep the badge on every screen.
- Wording: "bending at the waist", never spine claims. "Training record", never "WorkSafeBC approved". Canada has WorkSafeBC orders and penalties, not OSHA fines.

## 3. Pain points

Keep from the team's list: compensation and medical cost · absenteeism and short-staffing · turnover (say "shows investment in workers"; do not promise a retention number) · inconsistent onboarding · regulatory exposure (WorkSafeBC, not OSHA).

**Drop:** "builds stamina and boosts picking quotas". There is no evidence; an investor will ask for it.

**Add:**
1. **Training that doesn't work.** The Cochrane finding. This is the core insight.
2. **No proof.** After an injury, the owner cannot show who was trained, when, or how well.
3. **Hidden soreness.** Workers stay quiet until it becomes a claim. A private daily one-tap check surfaces it early. (The regulation also requires educating workers on early signs and symptoms.)
4. **No safety officer.** A 10–50 person business has an owner wearing every hat. It must run itself in three minutes.
5. **Hiring waves.** Seasonal and high-turnover sites onboard constantly. New and young workers are the highest-risk group.
6. **Language barriers.** A red/green skeleton needs no English.
7. **Premiums and COR.** Owners feel premiums every year; records that support a COR audit are worth up to 10% of them.

## 4. Business model and "why they keep coming back"

**Pricing: per worker, not flat.** $5 per worker per month, $49 monthly minimum. Benchmarks are $5–24 per user per month. A 20-worker business pays $100/month, about $1,200/year, against one ~$43,000 claim. Cost to serve is near zero because the AI runs in the customer's browser.

**Retention engine, in order of strength:**
1. **Daily habit tied to shift start.** Products used daily are not cancelled.
2. **Records lock-in.** The training history is the asset (inspections, COR audits, due diligence). It lives in LiftSafe.
3. **Quarterly certification** with due/overdue status.
4. **Turnover is demand.** Every new hire is a new onboarding.
5. **Streaks and site participation.** Light gamification; teams compete on participation, never on body scores.
6. **Weekly supervisor digest** ("3 overdue, 2 soreness flags, top issue: reach"). Roadmap.
7. **New movement packs** per industry. Roadmap.

## 8. Investor pitch (10 slides, ~8 minutes + demo)

1. **Hook.** "Who here has hurt their back lifting something?"
2. **Problem.** $2.35B in BC in five years. One claim ≈ $43K. Premiums follow.
3. **Insight.** Lifting training is proven not to work (Cochrane, 20,000 workers). It's a video once a year.
4. **Solution + live demo.** A judge does the warm-up and one lift. Skeleton, voice, score, snapshot. Then the dashboard with their check-in on it.
5. **Why now.** Pose AI runs free in any browser, on-device. Two years ago this needed a server and a contract.
6. **Market.** Top-down estimate and the bottom-up BC → Canada number.
7. **Business model.** $5 per worker per month. Near-zero cost to serve. Daily use, records lock-in.
8. **Competition.** The table in 2.4: verified · daily · self-serve · private.
9. **Traction.** Built in 48 hours. Validation result. Tester count. Customer quotes. Live URL as a QR code.
10. **Roadmap and ask.** Pilot with 3 Kamloops businesses to measure injury outcomes; packs per industry; time-clock integrations. The ask: pilot customers and introductions.

**Q&A answers to rehearse**
- *"Does it reduce injuries?"* "Not proven yet, and we won't pretend. Traditional training is proven not to. We measure technique and participation today; the pilot measures injuries."
- *"Isn't this surveillance?"* "No video leaves the device or is stored. Only scores. It coaches; it never blocks anyone from working."
- *"Vimocity?"* "Enterprise, utilities, video-based. Nobody verifies the movement. We're self-serve for the small businesses they don't sell to."
- *"Is it just Google's model?"* "Google finds the joints. The movement analysis, scoring, coaching and records are ours, with N tests and a validation study."
- *"Why won't they cancel?"* "Daily habit, their records live here, quarterly certification, and every new hire needs onboarding."
